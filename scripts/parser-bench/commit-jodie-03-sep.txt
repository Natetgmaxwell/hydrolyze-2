1. LC-specific warm-up

4 × 150 @ 2:30

Each as:

50 swim — establish long LC rhythm

25 scull + 25 swim — connect catch to stroke

50 descend within the 50

Then:

4 × 100 @ 1:40

25 build stroke rate

25 hold that rate without shortening

25 DPS

25 strong through the wall

The coaching point is "changing gears without losing stroke length".

Then:

4 × 50 @ 1:00

#1 — build 1–4 through the 50

#2 — 15 m fast breakout + settle

#3 — 25 strong / 25 easy

#4 — progressive to approximately 90%




2. LC Speed Development 

8 × 50 @ 1:30

15 m MAX + 35 m very easy

Alternate:

1.      Push

2.      Dive

3.      Push

4.      Dive etc.

Full acceleration. time the 15 m occasionally.

Not every swimmer, every repeat. Pick two or three athletes each time.

      

4 × 50 @ 2:00 — “Flying 20”

15 m build → 20 m MAX velocity → 15 m shut down

You aren't accelerating from a wall throughout the speed section.

You are reaching velocity and then trying to hold genuine swimming speed.

4 × 50 @ 1:30

25 race rhythm + 25 easy




But differentiate:

50/100: genuine 100-race stroke rate

200: 200-race rhythm

400–800: strong 200/400 rhythm

OW/tri: controlled fast turnover

4 × 50 @ 1:30

25 pursuit + 25 easy

Pair them.

Second swimmer leaves approximately 2 sec behind.

Race only to 25.

Then shut it down.

OW / Surf / Tri Variation

Tomorrow I would begin giving them a subtle October-specific variation.

Same main set as the distance swimmers, but make the 600s:

600 as:

200 aerobic

50 surge 

150 aerobic

50 surge

100 aerobic

50 strong

That's still predominantly aerobic.

We're simply teaching:

settle → change gear → settle again.

That's far more useful for their October racing than swimming a perfectly even 600 every time.

And importantly, the surge doesn't turn the entire repeat into threshold.

4. LC Finishing-Speed Test — 300 m

This is something I'd absolutely use tomorrow.

6 × 50 @ 1:30

First 35 m aerobic

Then:

final 15 m FAST

 I want you accelerating through the imaginary wall at 50 m.

No shortening.

No lifting the head at 47 m.

No coasting into the wall. teaching you to finish a LC length properly when already carrying aerobic fatigue.
